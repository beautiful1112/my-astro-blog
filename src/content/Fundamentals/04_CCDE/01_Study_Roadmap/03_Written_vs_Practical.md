# Written versus Practical

Both exams test **design judgment**. They differ in format, time pressure, and how deeply you must synthesize a single story.

## Side-by-side

| Dimension | Written (400-007) | Practical |
|---|---|---|
| Time | ~2 hours | ~8 hours (multi-module) |
| Form | Many short items / scenarios | Long multi-part design modules |
| Depth | Breadth across blueprint | Deep justification in fewer domains |
| Artifacts | Choose / order / short rationale | Diagrams, options, migration, defense |
| Book | Closed | Closed (scenario materials only) |
| Elective | N/A | Module 4 specialty track |

```text
Written:  many micro-decisions across the map
Practical: few big decisions, fully defended
Both:     R/C/A → options → trade-off → risk → migrate
```

## What Written rewards

- Fast recognition of **bad fits** (e.g. huge L2 for “simplicity”).
- Correct mapping of business words (RTO, sovereignty, opex) to tech.
- Knowing **when not** to use a protocol or overlay.
- Consistent plane thinking (control vs data vs management).

## What Practical rewards

- Holding one coherent design across modules without contradicting yourself.
- Explicit alternatives and losers.
- Migration that respects brownfield constraints.
- Ops and failure stories that match the HLD.

## Study split

| Phase | Written focus | Practical focus |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Blueprint coverage + misconceptions | Designer loop + cases |
| Mid | Timed item sets; weak domains | Full HLD packs with migration |
| Late | Mixed review + cards | Timed full modules + oral defense |

Do not wait until after Written to practice long scenarios—Practical muscle helps Written too.

## Real-world — candidate who aced labs, failed first Written

**Brief:** Strong CCIE; first CCDE Written weak on business and “which design fails this RTO.”

| R / C / A | Statement |
|---|---|
| R | Pass Written on second attempt within 4 months |
| C | Habit of diving to CLI and platform quirks |
| A | “More lab hours fix design exams” — false |

**Change:** Every study block ends with a one-sentence defense and a discarded option. Practical cases once per week even before Practical booking.

## Common traps

| Trap | Written symptom | Practical symptom |
|---|---|---|
| Feature shopping | Pick newest tech always | Incoherent multi-vendor stack |
| Ignoring ops | No “who runs it” | Unmaintainable HLD |
| Shared fate | Miss dual-homing illusion | Stretch L2 as “HA” |
| No migration | N/A item miss | Cannot phase cutover |

## Risks

- Over-preparing for Practical diagrams while Written breadth is thin.
- Treating Written as trivia; missing trade-off items.
- Changing design philosophy between modules in Practical.

## Interview framing

“Written checks whether I can choose fit designs quickly across domains; Practical checks whether I can live inside one design story under failure and migration pressure.”
